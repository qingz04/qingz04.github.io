About me

I am a senior undergraduate in Electrical Engineering at Purdue University. My research interests are in robotics, control, state estimation, autonomous systems, and perception, with current work involving state estimation and predictive control for ground and aerial vehicles.

I have experience with experimental hardware, quantum-optics instrumentation, and PCB design. I like knowing why something works, not just that it does. Having built parts of the sensor and hardware chains that measurements come from, my instinct when an estimate goes wrong is to trace the error back to its physical source before reaching for a more sophisticated filter. That habit—connecting sensing, modeling, estimation, and control—is the main perspective I bring to robotics research.

Outside of research, I enjoy meeting people and exploring opportunities beyond my working field. I am curious about how people arrive at different interests, careers, and ways of thinking, and I often find that a conversation with someone from a different background gives me a new way to look at my own work. At Purdue, I help organize career-related events for the Chinese Students and Scholars Association, which has given me opportunities to connect students with alumni and companies from different industries. I value these interactions not simply for networking, but for the stories and perspectives behind them.

Ortho-Mosaic Reconstruction from UAV Imagery under an Unreliable Pose Model
TypeResearch — Jul 2026, ongoing
LabPurdue Airlab
StackPython, OpenCV
SitePurdue University, West Lafayette, IN

A stitching pipeline for drone survey imagery — 1,145 frames across eleven flight strips into a 99-megapixel mosaic — where onboard heading and GNSS cannot serve as pose ground truth. Benchmarking showed the imagery survives stitching essentially intact; the residual error belongs to a pose model with no per-frame heading and no scale, and seam artefacts follow the baseline between frames rather than the time between them.
