About me

I'm a senior undergraduate in electrical engineering at Purdue College of Engineering. I came up building hardware — quantum-optics, RTL, PCB design — and I'm now moving into robotics, working on state estimation and predictive control for ground and aerial vehicles.

I like knowing why something works, not just that it does. Having built the sensor chains that measurements come from, my instinct when an estimate goes wrong is to trace the error back to its source before reaching for a better filter. That habit is the main thing I bring with me.

Away from the lab I play lead guitar — British rock, hard rock, punk metal — and I spent high school running a music club in Suzhou. At Purdue I help run career related events for the Chinese student association: holding career fairs, getting alumni in a room with students.

I am applying for research postgraduate study in robotics, control and state estimation, starting autumn 2027.

Selected Projects

3D Vehicle Dynamics State Estimation and NMPC Trajectory Tracking
TypeSenior Design — 2026, in progress
AdvisorProf. Ziran Wang, Purdue Digital Twin Lab
StackC++, ROS 2 Humble
SitePurdue University, West Lafayette, IN

Porting TUM FTM's 3D vehicle dynamics estimator — a 3D point-mass EKF supported by a single-track UKF — from a race car onto a research vehicle with a weaker sensor set, then closing the loop with an NMPC trajectory tracker so that the cost of estimation error can be measured rather than assumed.

Ortho-Mosaic Reconstruction from UAV Imagery under an Unreliable Pose Model
TypeResearch — Jul 2026, ongoing
LabPurdue Airlab
StackPython, OpenCV
SitePurdue University, West Lafayette, IN

A stitching pipeline for drone survey imagery — 1,145 frames across eleven flight strips into a 99-megapixel mosaic — where onboard heading and GNSS cannot serve as pose ground truth. Benchmarking showed the imagery survives stitching essentially intact; the residual error belongs to a pose model with no per-frame heading and no scale, and seam artefacts follow the baseline between frames rather than the time between them.

A Strategy-Driven Multi-Agent Control Framework for Autonomous Racing
TypePurdue VIP ARES Control Team — Spring 2026
AwardITS Michigan Scholar Award — undergraduate winner, CCAT Global Symposium poster competition
DOI10.5281/zenodo.19992669
SitePurdue University · University of Michigan

A layered strategy / planning / execution architecture for multi-agent racing. The low-level controller was carried through PID, ADRC, LQR and finally MPC, once the objective had to trade tracking error and control effort against strategy-dependent priorities. Team technical report, not peer-reviewed.
