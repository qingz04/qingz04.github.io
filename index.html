Research directions

Estimation

Vehicle state estimation

Sideslip angle and body-frame velocity are never measured directly, and GNSS can drop out for seconds. The estimator, not the controller, usually sets the ceiling.

Open questionHow much of an estimator's accuracy survives being moved from a race car onto an ordinary research vehicle with a weaker sensor set?
Control

Predictive control on real hardware

On an embedded PC, horizon length, model fidelity and solver time trade against each other — and a missed deadline is a safety problem, not a dropped frame.

Open questionWhich part of the state estimate — velocity, yaw, sideslip — actually dominates the closed-loop tracking error?
Geometry

Reconstruction under a wrong pose model

Aerial mapping inherits whatever the pose estimate got wrong. When onboard heading and GNSS are untrustworthy, the geometry degrades in ways that look like image-quality problems but are not.

Open questionWhen a mosaic degrades, how do you attribute the loss to the pose model rather than to resolution or blending?

Current work

Senior design · in progress

3D vehicle dynamics state estimation and NMPC trajectory tracking

Porting TUM FTM's 3D vehicle dynamics state estimator — a 3D point-mass EKF supported by a single-track UKF, in C++ on ROS 2 Humble — onto our research vehicle, then running an NMPC trajectory-tracking controller on top of it, so the effect of estimation quality on closed-loop tracking can be measured rather than assumed.

Advised by Prof. Ziran Wang.

Aerial mapping · ongoing

Ortho-mosaic reconstruction from UAV imagery

Building and evaluating a stitching pipeline for drone survey imagery, where the onboard heading and GNSS are not reliable enough to be used as pose ground truth. Roughly 99 megapixels of mosaic across eleven flight strips, benchmarked against a registered reference.

What the benchmark showed
  • Texture fidelity is essentially preserved through stitching — the loss is not in the imagery.
  • The binding constraint is the pose model: it carries no per-frame heading and no scale, so absolute scale needs ground control.
  • Seam artefacts track the baseline between contributing frames, not the time gap between them.

Selected work

A Strategy-Driven Multi-Agent Control Framework for Autonomous Racing

Zhishan Wang, Kaijie Zhu, Zheng Qing, Kieran Venkat Desireddi, Jacob Zhang, Brian Lee

Purdue VIP ARES Control Team · Spring 2026 Team technical report — not peer-reviewed Third of six authors

A layered strategy / planning / execution architecture for multi-agent racing. The low-level controller was developed through PID and ADRC, then moved to LQR and MPC once the objective had to trade tracking error, control effort and stability against strategy-dependent priorities. Evaluated in ROS simulation on the Shanghai circuit. I worked on the control side across two semesters.

Controller Design for an Autonomous Racing Vehicle

Poster, CCAT Global Symposium — 17 April 2026

ITS Michigan Scholar Award Undergraduate winner, student poster competition

Companion poster to the framework above, presenting the controller progression and the closed-loop results on the simulated circuit. Presented with the ARES control team; the poster carries a seven-person author list.
